I've been wondering, with much earnestness, what my reaction is to the massive societal shift that everyone doing knowledge work, and certainly everyone who's building software, is experiencing.

I've not arrived at the answer yet, because it's not just one question and it's not just one answer. But one conclusion I've come to is that it makes me want to think more. With so much more leverage, I want to act thoughtfully, and one of the best ways I know to sharpen my thinking is to write it down, reflect on it and refine it.

Both within the companies I've worked for and the communities I've been a part of, I've long favored doing and building over spending a lot of time just talking or thinking about what could be done. Doing has always been the hardest part - actually executing against an idea - so that's where I've sought out my challenges. But with so much of the doing now being taken out of our hands, I see the value of that wane.

Of course, as somebody who has gotten to lead a lot of people, having the doing taken out of my hands is nothing new to me. There are many different levels of "doing" to be found in giving direction and guidance. Doing that kind of "doing" excellently, also requires thought and clarity. Investing thinking and doing into that clarity has served me well in serving those I'm responsible for.

That's why I'm seeking out those new levels of doing, and the clarity they demand. Now what's great about doing and why I've always focussed on it, is the confrontation with reality. When you're just playing around with ideas nothing pushes back on you in unexpected ways. But once you actually try to form software into a particular shape or hand someone a product, that's when you really see what works.

Writing, in turn, has an unyielding propensity to point out the flaws in my reasoning. A stream of consciousness can feel very well-formed and logical. But our brains like to fill gaps or make connections based on our priors and experience without those actually being available to us when wanting to explain something. When I force myself to put things on the page, I'm both forced to fill those gaps and clarify those connections, but also give myself the space to sit with it and clarify for myself which version of these is actually the one I believe. **Writing is the doing of thinking.**

So I'll be thinking more, writing more and sharing more here.
