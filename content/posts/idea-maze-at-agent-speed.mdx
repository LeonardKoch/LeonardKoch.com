One of my favorite thought technologies in starting a startup is the Idea Maze.
It is all the different avenues you walk down, encountering dead-ends, redirects and treasures while exploring the problem you're trying to solve.

In building Glue I went through one hell of an Idea Maze, trying out different ways of representing data flow, execution flow,
how to visually represent these, trying sub graphs that were nested inside other nodes but visible, that were folded into separate views and so on,
all trying to find the right path through the maze within a formidable landscape of possibilities.
A lot of these ideas only failed when coming into contact with their first users, evoking confusion because of missmatched metaphores or lacking baselines of understanding I had assumed.

Others failed because they technically did not mesh with each other or because if logically thought through,
turned out to work against one another, leading to broken edge-cases.
**These are the parts of the idea maze I've been absolutely sprinting through lately.**
Agents are able to look at your ideas with a broad view, considering many of the technical and conceptual implications,
while having enough technical detail training data to evaluate your idea on a technical level.
An agent when asked about doing conditional branch reconciliation within a directed acyclic graph can quickly tell you,
that there will be cases that have no solution and bring you in the direction that you'll need to suss those out
via static analysis, so you can prevent the user from creating these states in their graph.
Prior to working with agents (like I did in the past in this example) you'd have to build it to find out or at least
sit down with a piece of paper, some colored markers and a serious amount of brain power to determine that these problems exist and how to solve them.

With a good prompt, the [/grill-me skill](https://www.aihero.dev/skills-grill-me) and a bit of time to read through options surfaced by the Agent, you can fly through that.
